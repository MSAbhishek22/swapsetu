SwapSetu 3D

A dead battery should not end a rider's day.

In Indian cities, thousands of e-rickshaw and delivery riders run on swappable batteries. When a battery dies mid-shift, the rider loses work until they find a swap station with a charged pack and no queue. SwapSetu is a working 3D model of that city. Riders drain, queue, swap and ride on — live, in your browser. Add a station where the model hurts, and watch the wait fall.

    How a swap works in the model

    1

    Riders drain

    Every rider carries one battery. Riding drains it. The small bar over each rider turns from green to red as the charge falls.

    2

    They pick a station

    At low charge, a rider heads for the nearest station that should have a charged pack. If that station is out of packs, they queue — and the wait is timed.

    3

    Swap and ride on

    The dead pack goes into the charger and a full one goes into the vehicle. The station slowly recharges its packs, so a busy station can run dry at peak hours.

    The point of the model is placement. Six stations cover this city unevenly, so one zone waits much longer than the rest. You can fix it yourself: switch on Add station, click a junction in the red zone, and watch the average wait fall.

    A swap network is only as good as its worst corner. SwapSetu makes that corner visible — and lets a planner test a fix in seconds instead of after a costly rollout.

    Honest limits. SwapSetu is a simple teaching model, not real Battery Smart data. Drain, recharge and demand follow plain rules written in the README, so the numbers show how placement behaves, not what any real station earns or waits today.
